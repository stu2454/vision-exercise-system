# Sit to Stand — instructions for testing

**Link:** <https://stu2454.github.io/vision-exercise-system/web/try/>

Send the link and this page. Everything below is written to be forwarded as it
stands.

---

## What this is

An early prototype that uses an ordinary webcam to watch someone stand up from
a chair and sit back down, and counts the repetitions. There are no sensors and
nothing to wear.

It takes about five minutes. It is not finished, and finding out where it fails
is the point of you trying it.

## Before you start

**Nothing you do is sent anywhere.** The camera picture stays inside your
browser tab. It is not uploaded, not saved to a file, and not seen by anyone.
The counting happens on your own computer too. Closing the tab switches the
camera off and leaves nothing behind.

You will need:

- **A sturdy chair without wheels**, ideally with a firm seat. Not a stool, and
  nothing that might slide.
- **Room to stand up safely**, with something stable within reach if you would
  like it.
- **Somewhere to put the computer about 2–3 metres away**, high enough that
  your whole body from head to feet fits on the screen. A table or shelf is
  better than the floor. A laptop lid angles the camera upwards, so you may
  need to prop the back of it up or tilt the screen forward.

**Please stop if you feel unsteady or unwell at any point.** Nothing here needs
finishing.

## Which browser

Chrome, Edge or Safari, on a Mac or Windows computer. A tablet will probably
work but has not been tested.

**On a Mac, turn off the camera effects first.** Recent macOS versions light the
edges of the screen whenever the camera is on, which changes the lighting on
you. Click the green camera icon in the menu bar once the camera has started and
switch off **Edge Light**, and **Centre Stage** if you see it.

## What will happen

1. **The page loads the software.** The first time this takes up to a minute,
   because it downloads the exercise engine. A spinner says what it is doing.
   After that it is quick.

2. **Your browser asks for the camera.** Allow it. Nothing works otherwise.

3. **You will see yourself**, with a green outline over your body.

4. **Position yourself.** Sit in the chair facing the screen. Large white words
   across the bottom tell you what to change:

   | It says | Do this |
   |---|---|
   | STEP INTO VIEW | It cannot see you at all |
   | MOVE FURTHER AWAY | Part of you is off the screen, usually feet or head |
   | MOVE A LITTLE CLOSER | You are too small in the picture |
   | MOVE TOWARDS THE MIDDLE | You are near the edge |

   When the words go away, you are in the right place. **Check this both
   sitting and standing** — the usual problem is that your feet drop off the
   bottom of the screen once you sit.

5. **Raise one arm to begin.** Either arm. Bend your elbow so your hand is up
   beside your head, and hold it there for about a second. A bar fills up to
   show it has seen you. Then put your arm down — you have three seconds before
   it starts counting.

6. **Stand up and sit down, at your own pace.** Stand up fully, pause for a
   moment, then sit back down with control. A large number counts each one.
   There is no target and no rush.

7. **Raise both arms to finish**, while still sitting. Hold for about a second.
   It stops and shows your total.

   **Do this before you get up to leave.** Standing up to walk away can be
   counted as another repetition.

## What we would like to know

Please tell Stuart anything you noticed. The failures are more useful than the
successes.

- **How many did you actually do?** Compare it with the number on screen. If
  they differ, that is exactly the sort of thing we need.
- **Did it count anything you did not do?** This matters more than a missed one.
- **Did it miss any?** If so, was there anything different about that one —
  slower, shallower, a pause?
- **Did the arm gestures work?** Did you have to try more than once?
- **Was the positioning guidance clear?** Did you know what it wanted?
- **Anything confusing, awkward or uncomfortable?** Including the wording.

If you feel like pushing it: try one deliberately half-hearted stand, or push up
off your thighs with your hands, or pause halfway up. Knowing what it does with
those is genuinely valuable.

## If something goes wrong

**"Could not start" or the camera never appears.** The camera was probably
blocked. Look for a camera icon in the address bar and allow access, then
reload. Check nothing else is using the camera — video calls hold on to it.

**It never counts anything.** Usually a positioning problem. Check the words at
the bottom of the picture and make sure they have gone while you are *sitting*,
not just standing.

**It counts far too many.** Please stop and tell Stuart what you were doing.
That is a fault worth catching.

**It is slow or the picture stutters.** Close other tabs, particularly video
calls. Tell us — how well it runs on ordinary computers is one of the things we
are trying to find out.

## Honest limitations

- It has only been tested on **one person, in one room**. You are the second.
- It counts a repetition when you stand up fully and sit back down. It cannot
  tell the difference between an exercise and getting up for any other reason.
- It does not detect using your hands or an armrest, and does not judge whether
  you are doing it well.
- **It is not a medical device.** It counts movements. It does not assess you,
  diagnose anything, or say whether an exercise is suitable for you.
